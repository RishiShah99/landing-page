RDR · 2026

Rollout-Decoded Reconstruction for long-horizon prediction in latent world models.

A latent world model's decoder trains on observation-anchored states but deploys on its own free-running rollout. RDR is one loss term that trains it where it is deployed. The full paper is on arXiv:2608.25017, archived at doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22004716.

1. Abstract

Latent world models train the decoder on posterior latents, computed while observations are available, then deploy it on a free-running rollout that drifts for tens or hundreds of steps without one. The two distributions diverge exactly where long-horizon behavior is decided.

Rollout-Decoded Reconstruction (RDR) free-runs the model during training, exactly as evaluation rolls it, decodes every rollout latent, and penalizes its error against ground truth. At weight λ = 0 it recovers the standard objective, so every comparison is a one-flag A/B.

The gap has stood open in every latent world model since PlaNet named it. RDR is an objective, not an architecture: it composes with any world model that retains a decoder.

  • One loss term, zero new parameters, training-time compute only.
  • Valid prediction time from 3.87 to 6.97 time units on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, a 1.80× improvement at an identical 193,568 parameters, with 10 of 10 preregistered configurations in the same direction at 1.71 to 2.50×.
  • Supervision, not capacity: a split decoder with 25.7% more parameters does worse in 7 of 10 configurations.
  • The mismatch is measured: at matched latent drift, RDR decodes with lower error.
  • The advantage grows with latent width in a descriptive sweep, and preliminary control experiments show robustness to planner–training rollout mismatch.

2. Introduction

Multi-step training signals in world models act in latent space, so the decoder never sees a multi-step rollout latent.

2.1 Why the gap stayed open

  • PlaNet named the fix and dropped it. Observation overshooting was set aside as too expensive in image domains, so its value was never established.
  • The Dreamer line never revisited it. PlaNet's latent overshooting and its successors' KL balancing stay in latent space; no imagined state is decoded as a training loss.
  • Decoder-free models such as TD-MPC2 and MuDreamer remove the component RDR trains, the one family it does not reach.
  • Exposure-bias methods fix the input, not the decoder. Scheduled sampling and professor forcing act on model-generated inputs, unrolled PDE surrogates have no decoder, and Self Forcing, Diffusion Forcing, and Next Forcing work in pixel space.

2.2 What is new

Rollout-LaSDI comes closest: it trains a latent reduced-order model's decoder on decoded rollouts and cuts maximum error threefold on 2D Burgers, but with regression-fit linear latent dynamics, a smooth system, no control result, and a fixed latent width. Rollout-decoded training is established for latent reduced-order models and, to the authors' knowledge, unmeasured in world models.

  • New: rollout-decoded training isolated in world models, on a chaotic system, at fixed parameters, data, seeds, and budget, with capacity ruled out by a matched control.
  • Not claimed: competition with architectural work. Symmetry reduction, discrete latents, KL balancing, and latent overshooting leave the decoder's training distribution unchanged; RDR applies on top of each.
  • Archived: every number traces to artifacts from a preregistered protocol, and the figure script fails the build on drift.

3. The RDR Objective

3.1 Model and base objective

A deliberately plain triplet keeps the objective the only thing under test: an MLP encoder (two hidden layers of 256), a four-layer S5 predictor (state size 64), and an MLP decoder Dψ (one hidden layer of 512) over 64-point fields u. An EMA target encoder (decay 0.999) supplies posterior targets. Four standard terms train it:

Term What it supervises
LTFteacher-forced one-step latent prediction
LRmulti-step latent rollout consistency, in latent space only
LOBSdecode the teacher-forced prediction
Lreconreconstruction anchor on the online encoder

None exposes the decoder to a multi-step rollout latent. That is the gap.

3.2 The RDR term

RDR decodes the free-running rollout ẑt+k that LR constrains and evaluation scores, with αe ramping from 0 to 1 over epochs 2 to 5:

LRDR = (1 / K) Σk=1K ‖ Dψ(ẑt+k) − ut+k ‖²,    L = LTF + αe LR + LOBS + Lrecon + λ LRDR    (1)

  • The gradient reaches all three components: the decoder directly, the predictor and encoder through the rollout chain.
  • λ = 0 is the baseline exactly (posterior-only); the operating weight is 0.3.
  • The only cost is training-time compute: K extra decoder evaluations per window and their backward pass. Inference is unchanged.

4. Forecasting on a Chaotic PDE

4.1 Setup

Kuramoto–Sivashinsky on L = 22 (64 grid points, ETDRK4, dt = 0.1) gives 512/64/64 train/validation/test trajectories of 256 snapshots. Training unrolls K = 128 steps at batch 64, Adam 3 × 10⁻⁴, three seeds per arm; the headline configuration is latent 32, decoder 512, λ = 0.3, 320 epochs. Valid prediction time (VPT) is the first time normalized RMSE crosses 0.5, in time units (tu), over 200-step and 1300-step horizons. One Lyapunov time is 23.26 tu.

4.2 Main result

  • Fresh seeds, never used in selection: posterior-only 3.87 ± 0.23 tu, RDR 6.97 ± 0.42 tu, a 1.80× improvement at an identical 193,568 trainable parameters.
  • Long horizon, same checkpoints: 3.77 against 6.90 tu.
  • Ten preregistered rows, ten wins: nine configurations plus the winner's fresh-seed rerun, at 1.71× to 2.50×.
  • The preregistered gate holds. The primary arm had to beat 5.77 tu, the observation-space baseline at an earlier budget, and reaches 6.47 ± 0.40 at fresh seeds, every seed above the bar.

4.3 Ablations

  • Supervision, not capacity. A split variant with its own rollout decode head (243,296 parameters against 193,568), fixed by preregistration as the primary arm, does worse than the shared decoder in 7 of 10 configurations on both horizons. Its 1.67× ratio is capacity-confounded.
  • The split is latent-size dependent: decisive at latent 128 with a linear decoder (VPT 0.43 without, 0.90 with, sharp-head evaluation), reversed at latent 32 with a 512-wide decoder.
  • The term is insensitive to λ: 0.1, 0.6, and 1.0 keep the capacity-matched ratio between 2.0× and 2.3×.

4.5 Diagnostics of the latent mismatch

  • Both arms drift off the posterior. Normalized distance to the true state's latent reaches 0.39 for posterior-only and 0.33 for RDR by 3.9 tu, and both saturate near 1.
  • RDR's decoder reads drifted latents better: at matched distance 0.3, field error is 0.28 against 0.38, and RDR is lower in 3 of 3 seeds at every sampled distance. Improved dynamics and a more robust decoder both contribute; the diagnostic does not divide the gain.

5. Control Experiments

On pendulum swing-up and a cartpole swing-up variant, ten-model ensembles per arm drive a cross-entropy-method MPC planner over 20 paired episodes. These results are preliminary; each comes with its matched control.

5.1 Step-efficiency

  • Fixed epochs: as data shrinks to 50/25/10%, RDR wins 20 of 20 paired episodes at every reduced rung on both tasks (pendulum margins +745, +1072, +396).
  • Matched optimizer steps narrow the margins to +3/+13/−18, reversing at two cartpole rungs. RDR reaches useful control in fewer steps; no sample-efficiency claim is made.

5.2 Robustness to planner mismatch

A planner that resets the recurrent state every step, unlike training, is easy to ship. Return gained by switching to the stateful planner:

Row Posterior gain RDR gain
pendulum, seeds 0–19+9.04+1.82
pendulum, seeds 100–119+18.12+3.59
cartpole, seeds 0–19+17.08+12.80
cartpole, seeds 100–119+12.71+10.64

The posterior arm gains more in every row, absorbing the mismatch as lost return; RDR, trained on free-running rollouts, is largely indifferent. Under the stateful planner the arms are equivalent (+0.61 and +1.24 on the primary rows), so RDR cuts the cost of the mismatch and adds no planning advantage once it is removed.

6. Scope

6.1 Observation-space comparison

The strongest non-latent control is an observation-space pushforward predictor trained on its own unrolled outputs at the winner's budget.

It reaches parity with the capacity-matched RDR arm, every gap far inside one seed-level standard deviation: on a fully observed, 64-dimensional system the latent bottleneck buys nothing at matched budget. This result bounds the bottleneck rather than the objective. The RDR contrast is within-latent throughout, so the class comparison leaves it unaffected.

6.2 Limitations and future work

  • Benchmark scope: one system at one domain size, three seeds per cell, a metric quantized at 0.1 tu, and two low-dimensional control tasks.
  • Training cost: +40% decoder evaluations and 5–10% wall-clock overhead per run at the headline configuration.
  • Mechanism: the gain is not divided among decoder robustness, improved latent dynamics, and implicit regularization.
  • Selection: sweep selection compared test-split results; validation re-ranking picks the same winner (ρ = 0.979).
  • Next: settings that force a latent (partial observability, pixel observations, compact-state planning), more systems and backbones, and gradient-pathway ablations.

7. Conclusion and Implications

RDR trains a latent world model's decoder on the rollout it actually decodes, with one loss term and no new parameters, and nearly doubles valid prediction time on a chaotic PDE. Any Dreamer-lineage model that retains a decoder can adopt it for one weight and training-time compute.

Paper: arXiv:2608.25017, archived at doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22004716. All training ran on short-lived cloud GPUs for roughly $155, about 29 H100-hours across 90 training runs and 210 evaluations; every evaluation and figure reruns on a laptop CPU.
