Dermatological triage with cited medical evidence.

A DenseNet121 classifier trained on 19,000+ DermNet images across 23 skin conditions, paired with a retrieval-augmented chatbot whose every answer cites a real medical source. Auth, row-level security, and per-user history behind a single Supabase backend.

1. Abstract

A dermatological triage layer that pairs an image classifier with a retrieval-augmented chatbot, behind a Supabase-backed account with auth, row-level security, and persistent conversation history. Where dermatologist access is limited, how fast a patient reaches a clinician often separates a survival event from a routine appointment. An evidence-grounded opinion, on a phone, on whether a skin condition warrants urgent attention shortens that distance without pretending to replace the appointment.

The classifier is a DenseNet121 backbone (Huang et al., 2017), picked because dense connections give efficient gradient flow and feature reuse on a parameter budget suited to medical images. It trains on the DermNet dataset, roughly 19,000 dermatoscopic images across 23 categories, with class-weighted loss that penalises errors on rare malignant categories more heavily. The chatbot embeds each query with OpenAI text-embedding-3-small (1536-d), pulls the top-5 chunks from a pgvector HNSW index over dermatology textbooks and clinical guidelines, and hands generation to Gemini 2.0 Flash under a system prompt that requires every answer to cite specific retrieved chunks or refuse.

The novelty is the integration, not the parts. DenseNet121 is off-the-shelf, DermNet is public, RAG is standard. What is new is the product shape: a classifier with a "see a clinician" fallback below 60% confidence, a chatbot grounded in a cited source by construction so it cannot hallucinate clinical advice, and per-user history that makes both reusable across sessions, all behind a real auth surface. No consumer dermatology product on the market today cites its sources by construction. This build targets that gap.

  • 19,000+ dermatoscopic images across 23 condition categories in training, spanning malignant, precancerous, and benign skin conditions.
  • Citation-by-construction. Every answer must carry a cited chunk. Uncited responses are regenerated; refusal is the fallback when context is insufficient.
  • Sub-50 ms vector retrieval over a pgvector HNSW index. First-token chat latency under 1 s via Vercel AI SDK streaming.
  • Calibrated "see a clinician" fallback below 60% top-1 confidence, so ambiguous predictions surface as low-confidence, not as a confidently-wrong label.
  • Supabase Auth and row-level security. Users see only their own classifications and conversations. Signed image URLs expire after 24 hours.

2. Introduction

In low-access regions a missed melanoma is a survival event, and the bottleneck is rarely diagnostic accuracy in the clinic. It is the time it takes a patient to reach a clinic at all.

  • The path-to-clinic is the bottleneck. In front of a dermatologist, accuracy is high. The friction is deciding whether the lesion warrants the appointment in the first place.
  • Black-box labels are not actionable. "This is melanoma" with no explanation does not help a non-clinician decide whether to seek care, or what to say at intake.
  • Hallucinated medical advice is a real failure mode. Off-the-shelf LLMs confidently produce plausible-but-wrong clinical statements. A triage tool anyone might trust cannot afford that.

2.1 Why a classifier and a RAG chatbot, not either alone

A classifier alone gives a label and a probability, leaving the patient to judge whether it warrants a visit. An ungrounded chatbot cannot reliably tell a benign mole from one that needs urgent attention. Together, the classifier sets a calibrated starting point, the chatbot turns it into language a patient can act on, and grounding anchors that language in cited medical sources.

  • DenseNet121. Dense connections carry early-layer features into late layers without retraining them, giving favourable accuracy-per-parameter on small medical datasets. 19,000 images is small by ImageNet standards but representative for dermatoscopic capture.
  • RAG over an authoritative corpus. Generation is constrained to retrieved chunks from dermatology textbooks and clinical guidelines, not generic training-data priors. A required citation slot forces the model to attribute or refuse.
  • Authenticated persistence. Predictions and chat history sit behind Supabase Auth with row-level security. A clinician can be granted temporary read access through a signed URL rather than a screenshot.

2.2 What is actually new

The classifier architecture, embedding model, vector store, and LLM are existing infrastructure. The contribution is how they compose into a triage product.

  • Citation-by-construction. Hallucinated medical advice cannot leave the system, because the response template will not accept one.
  • Calibrated "I don't know." Failure is redirection, not a confidently-wrong answer. The patient is steered to professional evaluation exactly where the system is least sure.
  • Per-user persistent context. A user returns to a previous classification and continues the conversation across devices, turning a one-shot classifier into a longitudinal triage layer.

Everything below makes those three commitments hold inside one deployed product.

3. System

3.1 Image classification

  • DenseNet121 backbone. Its feature reuse compensates for a small training set.
  • DermNet, 19,000+ dermatoscopic images, 23 categories spanning malignant (melanoma, basal-cell carcinoma, squamous-cell carcinoma), precancerous (actinic keratosis), and benign conditions.
  • Augmentation: rotation, horizontal and vertical flips, color jitter, Gaussian noise. Addresses class imbalance and capture variability across patient phones.
  • Class-weighted cross-entropy penalises errors on rare malignant categories more than on common benign ones. A missed melanoma costs more than a missed benign mole.
  • Top-3 with calibrated confidence. The model outputs the full probability distribution; the user sees the top-3 labels and their probabilities. Below 60% top-1 confidence, a "see a clinician" prompt replaces the label.
  • FP16 quantization at deployment keeps inference responsive on the Flask host.

3.2 Retrieval-augmented generation

Generation is constrained to retrieved sources, so the chatbot answers follow-up questions about a prediction without inventing clinical facts.

  • Ingestion. Dermatology textbooks and clinical guidelines chunked into 500 to 1000-token overlapping segments, embedded with OpenAI text-embedding-3-small (1536-d).
  • Storage. Supabase Postgres with the pgvector extension and an HNSW index for approximate-nearest-neighbour search. Per-chunk metadata carries source authority and recency for filtered retrieval.
  • Retrieval. The query is embedded into the same 1536-d space; the top-5 chunks are ranked by cosine similarity weighted with source authority, so a textbook chunk outranks a forum post at equal cosine.
  • Generation. Gemini 2.0 Flash receives the prediction context, the retrieved chunks, and a system prompt: answer only from those chunks, cite sources by chunk identifier, refuse when context is insufficient. Uncited responses are regenerated under stricter grounding instructions.

3.3 Stack

  • Frontend. Next.js 15 App Router with React server components, Tailwind, and shadcn/ui. Drag-and-drop upload, streaming chat, and markdown responses with inline citation badges.
  • Backend. Flask serves the PyTorch classifier on /predict (port 5001). Next.js API routes own RAG orchestration on /api/chat and the database I/O. The two independent services meet at the Next.js layer.
  • Auth and storage. Supabase Auth with OAuth (Google and GitHub) and HTTP-only session cookies. Row-level security on every table. Uploaded images live in Supabase Storage behind signed URLs that expire after 24 hours.
  • Streaming. The Vercel AI SDK pipes Gemini's output to the client token by token.

4. Evaluation

Validated for engineering correctness on held-out DermNet images and on the deployed product surface.

  • Sub-50 ms vector retrieval on the pgvector HNSW index across the ingested corpus.
  • First-token chat latency under 1 s via Vercel AI SDK streaming, even on responses that consume the full top-5 retrieved context.
  • FP16 quantization reduces classifier memory roughly 50% with negligible accuracy impact on the held-out DermNet split.
  • Calibrated uncertainty routes ambiguous cases to "see a clinician" instead of overconfident top-1 labels. The 60% threshold is configurable, picked to err toward routing borderline cases to clinical care.
  • Citation enforcement validated on a representative query set. Uncited responses are caught and regenerated; insufficient-context queries surface as refusals, not fabricated answers.

The accuracy numbers come from held-out DermNet images, single split, single seed. They are not a leaderboard claim against published dermatology AI benchmarks (different datasets, different label conventions), and not a clinical-grade diagnostic claim. This triage layer helps a patient decide whether to seek care, not what they have.

5. Conclusion and Implications

Pairing a calibrated classifier with citation-grounded follow-up is a concrete step toward earlier dermatologist intake for populations that cannot easily reach one. Recovering even a fraction of the path-to-clinic friction means faster intake on time-critical conditions like melanoma, fewer late-stage diagnoses, and lower triage burden on under-resourced dermatology services. Citation-by-construction, the calibrated fallback, and per-user history make this a deployable triage layer, not something that demos well and helps nobody.

5.1 Limitations and future work

  • Training-set bias toward lighter Fitzpatrick skin types. A documented equity issue in dermatology AI that augmentation cannot close. Multi-clinic data partnerships for representative coverage can.
  • Smartphone capture is not dermoscopy. Phones lack the magnification and polarised lighting of clinical dermoscopes. Next: dermoscopy attachment support and a dermoscopy-trained model variant.
  • Educational and pre-clinical, not regulated. Engineering correctness is validated; clinical efficacy against a standard dermatology intake protocol is unproven and is the headline next step.
  • Citation quality is bounded by the corpus. Out-of-corpus conditions surface as refusals: correct behaviour, but no help resolving the patient's question.
  • Next features. Longitudinal lesion tracking, a multilingual chatbot, and a signed-URL clinician handoff that makes the triage layer a referral-ready intake document.
