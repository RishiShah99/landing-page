Lethe · 2026

A contract-grade verifier for LLM-generated GPU kernels, and a native Blackwell backward for the gated-linear-recurrence family.

Twelve adversarial gates, aimed outward at 2,638 machine-generated kernels a public system had already accepted, and inward at the first native tcgen05 training backward for the GDN family. The full paper is on arXiv at arXiv:2608.12700 and archived at doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21563212.

1. Abstract

Language-model kernel generators report high correctness from one loose test: a few random inputs at one fixed shape, accepted if close to a reference. A kernel can pass and still return a finite number where the answer is NaN, change on every run, or break at a new shape. Lethe builds a contract-grade verifier of twelve adversarial gates, several tolerance-free, and aims it in two directions.

Outward, it audits 2,638 kernels a public system had already accepted. Inward, it checks the first native Blackwell tcgen05 training backward for the gated-linear-recurrence (GDN) family, whose correctness we establish independently against a double-precision oracle, so a pass shows the battery is not tuned to flatter its author.

  • Built a twelve-gate verifier with tolerance-free gates, derived thresholds, and a red team of nineteen broken kernels, all rejected while the honest reference passes.
  • Audited 2,638 accepted kernels: 62.1% carry a contract violation and 39.5% (1,043) fail a tolerance-free gate.
  • Exposed a one-way gap: KernelBench's standard test accepts 1,487 kernels the verifier rejects, against 14 in reverse.
  • Wrote the native backward at 3.3 × 10⁻³ relative error end to end, inside the 512-column tensor-memory budget the official Mamba-3 backward overflows in the open #904 bug.
  • Shipped six Triton Mamba-3 kernels that compile where the official backward trips #904. Full GPU suite: 1,191 passed / 3 skipped / 0 failed on B200.

2. Introduction

Kernel-generation benchmarks are only as trustworthy as the allclose test beneath them. Sarkar argues on twenty-four hand-built kernels that loose checks accept broken ones, but reports no rate on a real corpus. The Kernel Contracts taxonomy enumerates correctness classes; that taxonomy is theirs. Ours, to the best of our knowledge, is its first runnable, at-scale operationalization, audited on thousands of accepted, real kernels and passed by a certified native artifact.

2.1 One recurrence, five models

Five sub-quadratic sequence models (linear attention, GLA, Mamba-2, KDA, and GDN) replace attention with a fixed-size recurrent state and form one family. Their superset is the gated DeltaNet (GDN) update of a matrix state S from keys kt and values vt, read by queries qt, with log-decay gt, erase bt, and write wt gates:

St = (I − kt(bt ⊙ kt)⊤) Diag(egt) St−1 + kt(wt ⊙ vt)⊤,    ot = St⊤qt    (1)

Equation (1) adds the delta rule (store the correction to what S predicts, not the raw value) to channel-wise decay. Fixing its gates recovers the other four, and roughly 80% of the parameterization is shared, so one backward through (1) yields training gradients for all five. Each reduction is checked against its own reference.

2.2 What is new, and what is not claimed

  • Two firsts: a native Blackwell tcgen05/TMEM training backward for the GDN family, and a native reverse-state backward, the stage others still run in fla Triton.
  • Not claimed: the first native backward kernel for the family (cuLA's WY stage predates ours), the first non-Triton GDN/KDA backward (tilelang exists), or bitwise parity with fp64 (it is fp64-tight).

3. The Verifier and the Rigor-Gap Audit

3.1 The twelve gates

Each gate is an adversarial contract graded against a high-precision reference that refuses to run in reduced precision.

Gate Property checked
CMP-01correct on random and adversarial inputs (zeros, 10⁶, 10⁻⁶, denormals, long L)
CMP-02correct gradients, not only outputs (autograd versus finite difference)
CMP-03correct across batch, length, and width
ORD-01reordered summation within a derived ∝ √N rounding bound
ORD-02byte-identical across five repeats, no shared-buffer aliasing
ORD-03correct on an input built to expose a bad summation order
PRC-01correct in fp32, fp16, and bf16
PRC-02fed fp16, still keeps an fp32 running total
EXC-01infinities and NaNs match the reference in position and sign
EXC-02subnormal flush-to-zero matches the reference
RES-01output on the same device as the input
RES-02compiled kernel fits hardware limits (registers, shared memory, TMEM)

Tolerances are derived, not chosen (ORD-01: atol ≈ 4ε√N · scale), inputs are seeded, and the nineteen red-team kernels each embody one trick, from cached answers to swallowed infinities.

3.2 The audit

The corpus is Dr. Kernel / KernelGYM (MIT license): 8,920 fine-tuning trajectories, each ending in a Triton kernel. Its SSM-adjacent classes yield 3,134 kernels, each run sandboxed on a B200; keeping those with a positive recorded speedup and a floating-point input, minus toolchain artifacts, leaves 2,638. Five fairness rules push every ambiguous case toward the candidate.

  • Stripping every tolerance-arguable gate still leaves 54.7% in violation, against the 62.1% headline.
  • The 39.5% floor unions five channels no tolerance can excuse: EXC-01, ORD-02, output aliasing, CUDA errors during gating, and non-artifact failures to run.
  • The modal defect is EXC-01, at 34.2%: an ordinary number where the reference returns NaN or infinity, turning a training-time error into silent data corruption.

3.3 Four independent defenses

Is the checker just stricter? Four separately falsifiable answers:

  • Positive control. Our six Triton kernels pass every applicable gate, after the battery caught a real channel-check gap in one of them (fixed in 7f46226).
  • Threshold calibration. Every band-gate separates honest noise from real error; the one thin margin, ORD-03 at 1.2×, is the deliberately relaxed gate.
  • KernelBench's own harness agrees with our replica of its check on 98.5% of 1,030 pairs.
  • Hand-audit. Of 31 disputed cases, 16 are genuinely broken, 8 real but tolerance-dependent, and 7 dropped as out of scope.

3.4 The differential

KernelBench's paper-era check (allclose, atol=rtol=10⁻², five trials, fixed shapes) accepts 93.7% (2,472 of 2,638).

Ours: PASS Ours: FAIL
KernelBench: PASS 985 1,487 (918 tolerance-free)
KernelBench: FAIL 14 152

The checks disagree almost entirely in one direction: a systematic blind spot, not a difference in strictness. Under KernelBench's hardened fp32 tolerance of 10⁻⁴, 1,263 kernels still pass theirs and fail ours.

3.5 Robustness

A 300-kernel re-audit on torch 2.11 returns 68.6%, and the native-CUDA Sakana AI CUDA Engineer archive (213 kernels) shows a 20.2% environment-robust residual, mostly shape rigidity. The gap is neither toolchain-bound nor Triton-specific.

4. The Native tcgen05 Backward

4.1 The two hard kernels

The backward walks 64-step chunks in reverse, and two stages are hard:

  • K#1, the reverse inter-chunk state scan, carries the fp32 state gradient across chunks with a rank-one delta-rule correction per step. At dv = 64 it is one fused kernel under a single tensor-memory reservation.
  • K#2, the WY / triangular-inverse VJP, reuses the forward's T = (I + M)⁻¹ in four triangular products and never re-inverts.

4.2 Clearing the tensor-memory constraint

Blackwell's tcgen05 tensor core keeps operands in Tensor Memory (TMEM), capped at 512 columns and managed by hand. In state-spaces/mamba#904, the official Mamba-3 backward requests 544 columns at its normal performance setting, so it fails to compile and falls back to a path reported 38.7× slower on GB200; we reproduced it on our B200. Our first multi-GEMM kernels crashed by releasing TMEM per matmul, which the hardware forbids. Porting the single-reservation lifecycle of NVIDIA's mamba2_ssd.py cleared it.

4.3 Verification

  • fp64 pins: the forward reference matches an independent fp64 implementation to 5.9 × 10⁻¹⁷ to 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁶.
  • Full backward: 3.29 × 10⁻³ (scalar) and 3.31 × 10⁻³ to 3.67 × 10⁻³ (channel-wise) against the fp32 reference, under a 5 × 10⁻³ bound. At d_v = 128 the save-forward and default native paths differ by 5.21 × 10⁻³, the one configuration outside it.
  • It trains: all five members, with the fallback disabled. Four clear the training gate in 300 steps at L = 256; LA only at L = 64 over 4000 steps.
  • It passes the battery, clean on every tolerance-free channel.

4.4 Speed, stated plainly

The native backward is slower than the Triton flash-linear-attention library, roughly 8× at L = 512 and about 78× at L = 2048, because the reverse scan is sequential and fp32 glue dominates. Against our own earlier pipeline, channel-wise fusion cut the captured backward 2.75× and tensor-memory tiling cut the d_v = 128 variant 2.98×. Speed is future work.

4.5 The bridge

The gates that sink the most foreign kernels also caught our own bugs: our kernel twice produced non-finite values and propagated them, which exposed both bugs (EXC-01); CMP-03 caught the channel gap in §3.3; RES-02 detects the #904 constraint. A checker that flattered its author would not have caught the author's own bugs.

5. Supporting Results

  • Six Mamba-3 Triton kernels with no tl.dot run at num_warps 2/4/8, where the official kernel loses every ≥ 4 configuration; against it they run about 4× slower (0.22 to 0.36×), the cost of avoiding tl.dot. Priority on the MIMO backward is TileLang's.
  • A 1.1B-parameter Mamba-1-style model, trained from random initialization on PTB-XL across eight Blackwell GPUs through our fused kernel, reaches 0.880 macro-AUC with zero NaN: a stack demonstration, not a domain result.
  • RL: config-RL beats the shipped default 1.167× at long sequences, rediscovering our shape-gated selector; edit-RL is a negative, with no edit in 320 attempts beating the baseline.

6. Conclusion and Implications

Every reported result in GPU-kernel generation rests on a correctness test too weak to carry it. One verifier, one standard, aimed both ways, exposes the gap and passes a backward certified independently. A small set of tolerance-free contracts (non-finite propagation, determinism, shape polymorphism) would close most of it at modest cost.

The full paper is on arXiv at arXiv:2608.12700 and archived at doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21563212.

6.1 Limitations and future work

  • One primary corpus; the CUDA corpus shows a weaker effect.
  • Two idle gates (CMP-02, RES-02) on a forward-only corpus.
  • A narrow native envelope: d_k = 128, chunk = 64, d_v ∈ {64, 128}, one GPU generation.
  • Speed levers: a tl.dot path once Triton ships the #9093 tensor-memory fix, and less fp32 glue.
  • Peer review at an ML systems venue.
